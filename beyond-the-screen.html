Beyond the Screen: Film Tourism for Seoul

In 2016, Seoul was appearing in Korean dramas and films watched across Asia and beyond, but there was no official way for visitors to find the places they had seen on screen. This independent study proposed a government-backed film tourism program for Seoul, with a five-year marketing strategy and an official website and app for filming locations.

  1. The problem

    Strong Korean Wave exposure, but no film tourism infrastructure. Visitors relied on scattered blogs and fan forums.

  2. What I did

    Benchmarked New Zealand and the UK, analyzed Seoul's market, and wrote a five-year strategy and platform concept.

  3. The result

    A full proposal and marketing plan, anticipating the K-content travel boom that followed.

01Why film tourism

Film-induced tourism draws visitors to places they first saw on screen. The Lord of the Rings turned New Zealand into a destination, contributing to nearly 10% of its GDP and one in ten jobs. Seoul had the screen time, with visitors to Korea rising from 6.0 million in 2005 to 13.2 million in 2015, but no program to turn that attention into visits.

07Lessons learned

Read the weak signals

Fan-mapped drama locations hinted at demand before any official data did.

Policy and product together

A strategy only works with a tool travelers will actually use.

Borrow proof

International cases made a new idea credible to decision-makers.
