Beat, Meet, Lose: Fixing Why We Lost on Price

Every day, Expedia compared its hotel prices with Agoda, Booking.com and local Korean travel sites. The dashboard could show that Expedia was more expensive, but not why. Finding the reason was slow, manual detective work. I led a project that gave the team a shared code for every pricing problem and a playbook for fixing each one. It started in Korea and became part of Expedia's global dashboard.

  1. The problem

    Associates investigated each lost price by hand. About five issues were escalated a day; only three got fixed.

  2. What I did

    Logged ~690 real cases, sorted them into coded categories, and wrote a fix-it playbook for each code.

  3. The result

    More cases solved, a higher success rate, and a framework Expedia rolled into its global BML dashboard.

01Beat, meet or lose?

For every hotel, Expedia's price for the same room and date was compared with each competitor.

BEAT

Expedia is cheaper than the competitor.

MEET

Expedia matches the competitor's price.

LOSE

Expedia is more expensive, and travelers book elsewhere.

02What this project shows

  • Turning scattered, anecdotal reports into structured, measurable insight
  • Combining market research, data analysis, workflow design and stakeholder management
  • Running an 8-month initiative while keeping daily KPIs on track
  • Influence without authority: a local idea adopted by APAC and Global HQ

03The problem

Each Market Associate worked with an Account Manager on one region of Korea. Associates watched prices daily and dug into each "lose"; Account Managers negotiated the hard fixes with hotels. The old workflow had three manual steps: check the dashboard, check the live sites to find the cause, then escalate by email.

04Thought process

I worked backwards from one question: why do we keep losing on price? Each answer led to the next question, and each decision came from evidence the team collected together.

QuestionWhat we foundDecision
01Why do we lose on price?
Nobody could say. Reasons lived in emails and people's heads, and each case started from zero.
Log every case in one shared sheet instead of email.480 cases in 5 weeks
02Do the reasons repeat?
Yes. A handful of causes explained most losses, and the same ones showed up for every associate.
Name the 13 most common issues with keyword labels, then keep logging.+210 cases in 2 weeks
03Can every case be solved the same way?
The same issue had the same best fix, and the same owner: associate or account manager.
Group into categories with codes, and write a playbook entry for each.12 categories · 14 subcategories
04Is this only a Korea problem?
Japan, Taiwan, Singapore and Australia saw the same gaps, especially from local OTAs.
Pilot across APAC, refine the codes, and hand the framework to Global.Official BML dashboard, Sep 2019

How I drafted the codes

  1. Draft 1Log the casesA shared sheet: date, associate, hotel, competitor site, case category, issue, status.
  2. Draft 2Review the categoriesSort every issue by what was lost (rate or availability), whether it was fenced, and who we lost to.
  3. Draft 3Create #codesOne short hashtag per issue, so cases could be tagged, counted and tracked over time.

07Lessons learned

Data drives influence

Hard numbers turned a local efficiency idea into a global priority.

Validate across regions

Testing with four markets reframed the problem as APAC-wide, not Korea-specific.

08Next time

  1. Prototype earlier. A clickable dashboard mid-project could have won HQ over sooner.
  2. Bring in more markets early, such as India and Southeast Asia, so it never looked Korea-centric.
