3.2 · FAQ Chatbot · Expedia Group

FAQ Chatbot in a Local Messaging App

In 2019, Expedia's growth in Korea depended on small lodgings: motels, guesthouses and family-run rentals, often run by owners in their 60s and 70s. They struggled with Expedia's self-service tools and called their account managers for every small question. I led a KakaoTalk chatbot, inside the messaging app Korea already uses every day, that answered those questions automatically.

  1. The problem

    Partners signed up quickly but dropped off when operations got confusing, and routine calls ate up account managers' time.

  2. What I did

    Logged every partner call for four weeks, turned the patterns into a Q&A library, and built the bot in KakaoTalk.

  3. The result

    Routine support calls fell by 70% within a month of launch, freeing the team to sign new partners.

02The problem

Every major hotel in Korea was already on Expedia, so growth had to come from smaller properties. Local competitors onboarded these owners by phone. Expedia asked them to manage everything through a global web portal and email support, which many found hard to use.

03Research & validation

For four weeks, the whole team logged every call from new partners in a shared sheet. More than 200 calls, many with several questions each, became 300+ entries that we grouped into 12 categories with 4 subtopics each. The same themes appeared for all four account managers.

200+partner calls logged over four weeks
80%of calls were the same recurring questions
70%of routine questions a bot could answer

Why it mattered

  • It showed the problem was operational, not just partner inexperience.
  • It gave leadership a clear business case, measured in account managers' time.
  • It became the knowledge base the conversation flows were built on.

06Challenges

No engineering support

The whole solution had to be built with tools the team could run ourselves.

Partners liked calling

We positioned the bot as faster and available around the clock, and adoption followed.

07Lessons learned

Adapt locally

Korean partners needed more guidance than U.S. partners. Meeting them in KakaoTalk made support feel familiar instead of foreign.

Protect growth time

Structured, repeatable answers cut churn risk and gave account managers back the time to grow the business.

08Next time

  1. Measure retention. Track churn over time to quantify long-term impact.
  2. Share earlier. Bring results to other APAC teams sooner for feedback and wider adoption.
  3. Go beyond text. Add step-by-step onboarding guides or short video tutorials.
