Attention, Please!: A Poster Series for Amnesty International

What makes someone stop, think, and carry an idea away from a piece of paper they only walked past? I designed three posters for Amnesty International, each built on one image: the thing that causes harm turns into the one it harms.

  1. The challenge

    Make a static poster support a cause without over-explaining it, and still lead people to act.

  2. What I did

    Narrowed Amnesty's 20+ causes to three, tested concepts with viewers, and rebuilt the series around cause and consequence.

  3. The result

    A consistent three-poster series with a clear reading order and a visible call to action.

02The question

I started by comparing two kinds of posters: one that lets the viewer make the connection, and one that says exactly what to think. That became my question: should a poster be read in a second, or leave room to interpret?

Starting from the word "control", I narrowed Amnesty's causes to three: corporate accountability, gun violence, and technology.

03The turning point

First concept · Word as object

SPILL, SHOOT, MONITOR: each keyword shaped into an object.

Clear, but flat. Viewers recognized the topics but missed the context, the hierarchy, and what to do next.

→

Final concept · Cause becomes consequence

  • Oil spill becomes an oil-covered bird
  • A gun becomes a child's profile
  • Phone light becomes a hand over a woman's mouth

Each poster now leads the eye in the same order:

ImageConsequenceEvidenceAmnesty's positionTake part

04Refining the system

Peer critique said everything had equal weight, so nothing stood out. I tested in black and white first, then made these changes:

  • One warm color per poster, yellow type across the series
  • Heavier title type and natural line breaks
  • Statistics tied to one argument each
  • A visible "Take Part" call to action
  • The same stroke weight in every illustration
  • A bigger logo and URL, no drop shadows

05What I learned

Consistency is a system

Posters can differ in color and composition and still belong together through shared logic.

Hierarchy is a decision

When everything is emphasized, nothing is. Small shifts in weight guide how people read.

Clarity comes from removal

What I left out mattered as much as what I kept.
