3.1 · Player Redesign · Panopto

Player Modernization & Design Initiative

Panopto makes video software that companies use to share recorded meetings and training. In 2023, its biggest customer in Asia-Pacific, SK Group, was close to leaving because the video player felt outdated, especially on phones. I led the project to fix it, working with Panopto's product team in the U.S.

  1. The problem

    SK's employees struggled to watch videos on their phones, and a contract worth 9% of APAC revenue was at risk.

  2. What I did

    Asked users what they needed, narrowed it to three fixes, and worked with the U.S. team to build them.

  3. The result

    Two of the three fixes shipped in five months, and SK renewed its contract.

02Design goal

Panopto was built around education (lecture capture, note-taking, archiving), so its mobile experience had evolved more slowly than its competitors'. For enterprise users, mobile playback was the main barrier to adoption. SK asked for three features:

01
Manual video quality control

Quality followed bandwidth only, so heavy mobile data use drove complaints.

Shipped
02
Captions that scale to the screen

Caption size stayed fixed at desktop scale, so on phones it covered much of the video.

Shipped
03
Smart Seek with thumbnail previews

Without previews, scrubbing a long recording was guesswork.

Requested · not shipped

Why these features mattered

  • Closed the gap with Vimeo, YouTube and Netflix
  • Gave mobile viewers more control
  • Reassured executives who were evaluating alternatives
  • Restored trust by showing feedback would be acted on

03Research & validation

I surveyed 3 executives, 9 video platform managers and 50 high-engagement end users. With a 68% response rate, the results made the case for narrowing scope: renewal depended on immediate playback fixes, not a roadmap update ten months out.

04Alignment & delivery

Thirteen weekly alignment meetings with the U.S. Product team, March to July, with SK kept in the loop at kickoff, a week-10 demo and a July executive review at SK headquarters. Because HQ was balancing competing projects, I brought in the APAC VP and the CPO to keep momentum. I framed the Smart Seek deferral as a resource decision that could be revisited, and SK approved the renewal ahead of budgeting.

06Lessons learned

Cross-regional collaboration

Aligning teams across APAC and the U.S. made the difference. With urgency, transparency and regular communication, a distributed team could act as one.

Specific needs, broader strategy

I balanced the immediate renewal risk with long-term roadmap discipline, positioning SK's requests as improvements for all enterprise customers instead of one-off fixes.

07Next time

  1. Research wider. Include more enterprise customers to confirm which features matter across the market.
  2. Set expectations earlier. Align executives on roadmap trade-offs up front to ease early pressure.
  3. Bring in Product leadership sooner to speed up resource decisions.
  4. Prototype earlier so customers can see progress sooner.
  5. Write the playbook. Document the process so other high-stakes renewals can follow it.
The project secured a critical renewal, influenced product direction and rebuilt executive trust under pressure, and it showed where the approach could scale across other enterprise accounts.
