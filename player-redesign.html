3.1 · Player Redesign · Panopto

Player Modernization & Design Initiative

I led the modernization of Panopto's embedded video player for SK Group, Panopto APAC's largest enterprise customer, when an outdated design, missing features and weak mobile support put the renewal at risk. Working directly with the U.S. Product and Development teams, we shipped two of three customer-validated features in five months and secured the renewal.

TimelineMar – Aug 2023
RoleProject Lead: research, concept, validation, cross-regional coordination, customer communication
TeamMyself, HQ Product & Dev teams
ToolsFigma, MS Office, Salesforce, Jira, Confluence, Google Workspace

01What this project shows

  • Cross-regional collaboration grounded in user-centered methods
  • Turning customer pain points into shipped product updates
  • Prioritizing under resource constraints while protecting a renewal
  • Bridging cultural, language and time-zone gaps between SK and U.S. teams

02Design goal

Panopto was built around education (lecture capture, note-taking, archiving), so its mobile experience had evolved more slowly than its competitors'. For enterprise users, mobile playback was the main barrier to adoption. SK asked for three features:

01
Manual video quality control

Quality was set automatically by bandwidth, so heavy mobile data use drove complaints. Viewers needed a manual quality button.

02
Captions that scale to the screen

On mobile, captions stayed desktop-sized and covered much of the video, hurting translated content.

03
Smart Seek with thumbnail previews

Without previews while scrubbing, finding a moment in a long video was guesswork.

Manual video quality control

Scaled caption font size

Progress bar with thumbnail previews

Why these features mattered

  • Closed the gap with Vimeo, YouTube and Netflix
  • Gave mobile viewers more control
  • Reassured executives who were evaluating alternatives
  • Restored trust by showing feedback would be acted on

03Research & validation

I surveyed 3 executives, 9 video platform managers and 50 high-engagement end users. With a 68% response rate, the results made the case for narrowing scope: renewal depended on immediate playback fixes, not a roadmap update ten months out.

70%dissatisfied with the embedded player
80%rated Panopto outdated vs. Vimeo, YouTube and Netflix
45 · 35 · 20% priority for Quality Control, Captions, Smart Seek

04Internal alignment

Thirteen weekly alignment meetings with the U.S. Product team, March to July. Because HQ was balancing competing projects, I brought in the APAC VP and the CPO to keep momentum. Progress lived in Jira and Confluence, and deliverables met SK's budget deadline.

Wk 1–2Request alignment and scoping; customer survey
Wk 3Survey results and feature prioritization
Wk 4–7Progress checks and resource planning
Wk 8–10Prototype validation, feasibility checks, mid check-in with SK
Wk 11–12Customer feedback and refinement
Wk 13+Finalization and delivery readiness

05Customer validation

Kickoff in March, survey results in week 2, a mid-check demo in week 10, and an executive review at SK headquarters in July. I explained the Smart Seek deferral as a resource-driven decision that could be revisited, and SK approved the renewal ahead of budgeting.

06Resolution

Quality control Shipped

High, Medium, Low and Automatic options, addressing data-cost concerns in Korea, where fast networks defaulted to high bitrate.

Responsive captions Shipped

Captions scale to mobile screens without blocking content, reaching parity with desktop and improving accessibility.

Smart Seek Deferred

Documented as a roadmap candidate for broader enterprise demand.

07Outcomes

2 of 3priority features delivered in five months
9%of APAC bookings protected by the renewal
1feature kept open for the future roadmap

08Lessons learned

  • Cross-regional collaboration is built on urgency, transparency and steady communication.
  • Framing customer-specific fixes as scalable improvements wins support for the broader enterprise market.
  • Next time: broader enterprise research, earlier executive expectation-setting, earlier Product leadership engagement, earlier prototypes, and a repeatable playbook for high-stakes renewals.
